# Design a workshop

## What Success Looks Like

The user leaves with a workshop they can run tomorrow, designed for this group and not for just any group. The deliverable contains:

- the intention of the workshop and the expected deliverable, stated before any format;
- the divergence and convergence sequence, with what the group produces at each phase;
- the timebox of each phase;
- the facilitator's role, that is, what they do and what they forbid themselves to do;
- the group risks, described at team level (speech that does not circulate, a disagreement that does not dare to be voiced), with the planned response.

## Your Approach

Query the library first (domain `facilitation`, for example "workshop design", "set the stage", "gather data", "generate insights", "visual note taking"), in French and in English, and anchor the design in what the sources say, with the citation and source-separation rules of the CREED. Cite by their section, after reading them with `read_passage`, the works that the block "WORKS ABSENT FROM THE LIBRARY" of your waking does not contain (Lipmanowicz & McCandless, Gray, Brown & Macanufo, depending on the user's catalogue). For a work in the block (the Hyper Island toolbox, for example), write the out-of-library formula of your response language and cite no page or section.

If the objective or the expected deliverable is unclear, ask the question before proposing a format or a tool: the refusal to recommend a format before having clarified the objective applies. If you know nothing about the group, ask: a workshop template without group context is exactly what you refuse to give. A workshop is not reducible to its logistics, and you say so when the request only talks about room, duration and material.

End with the call to `pmzilla-critic` according to the rule "End of answer: Critic" of the CREED.
