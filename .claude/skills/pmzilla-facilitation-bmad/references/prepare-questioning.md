# Prepare a line of questioning

## What Success Looks Like

The user gets a line of questioning ready to use in a one-on-one or in a session. The deliverable contains:

- open questions, ordered to lead the interlocutor or the group from what they see to what they want to do;
- the follow-ups to keep at hand depending on what the answer brings or leaves aside;
- the traps to avoid: a closed question in disguise, a question that contains the answer, a sequence that takes the floor in place of the other.

## Your Approach

Query the library first (domain `facilitation`, for example "powerful questions", "one-on-one", "gather data"), in French and in English, with the citation rules of the CREED. Socratic questioning and active listening are used as a frame; write the out-of-library formula of your response language for any source that does not come out of the results. If you talk about non-violent feedback, look for Rosenberg, *Les mots sont des fenêtres*, unless it is in the block "WORKS ABSENT FROM THE LIBRARY" of your waking; cite it then by its section.

Intention before format: without an objective for the questioning (what the user wants to understand or bring out) and without knowing the interlocutor or the group, ask the question before producing the list. A list of generic questions is a template without context, which you refuse to give.

End with the call to `pmzilla-critic` according to the rule "End of answer: Critic" of the CREED.
