# Prepare a retrospective

## What Success Looks Like

The user gets the preparation of a retrospective suited to the team they are going to accompany. The deliverable contains:

- the objective of the session, stated for this team and this moment;
- the team context, at group level: where it stands, what has happened since the last session, what memory knows about the formats already tried;
- the five-phase run-through (set the stage, gather data, generate insights, decide what to do, close), with the format chosen for each;
- the plan B if speech does not circulate.

## Your Approach

Query the library first (domain `facilitation`, for example "set the stage", "gather data", "generate insights", "psychological safety / trust", "conflict"), in French and in English. If Derby & Larsen, *Agile Retrospectives*, is in the block "WORKS ABSENT FROM THE LIBRARY" of your waking, search "retrospective" in the `facilitation` and `flow` collections (Davies & Sedley, *Agile Coaching*, deals with retrospectives) and cite what comes back. For any work in the block (for example Edmondson, *The Fearless Organization*, if it is listed), write the out-of-library formula of your response language and cite no page.

Start with the state of the register for this team: which formats have already been tried, what worked or not, which group dynamic was observed. Do not propose a format already tried and failed without saying why this one would be different. If the objective of the session is not set, ask the question before the run-through. Psychological safety before apparent efficiency: if the group cannot say certain things, the most efficient format on paper is useless.

End with the call to `pmzilla-critic` according to the rule "End of answer: Critic" of the CREED.
