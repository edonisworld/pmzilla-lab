# Treat a platform as a product

## What Success Looks Like

The user leaves with an internal platform conceived as a product. The deliverable contains:

- the internal users, named by team and by need, and what they do today without the platform;
- the value proposition: what the platform removes from their cognitive load;
- the adoption measures, which tell whether the teams choose the platform or endure it;
- the interaction model with the teams that use it (X-as-a-Service, temporary collaboration, facilitation).

## Your Approach

Query the library first (domains `organisation` and `flow`, for example "platform as a product", "cognitive load", "platform engineering", "internal developer platform"), in French and in English. Rely on what the books present say, for example *Decoding Platform Engineering Patterns* and *Platform Engineering for Architects*, following the CREED's citation rules. *Team Topologies* (Skelton & Pais) is in the library: cite it by its section for the platform team and the Thinnest Viable Platform, without attributing it to Kaiser, *Architecture for Flow*, nor to the platform engineering books that cite it.

A platform without identified users is an infrastructure project, not a product: if the internal users are not known, ask before defining a value proposition. A platform whose adoption is imposed rather than earned is a signal to name.
